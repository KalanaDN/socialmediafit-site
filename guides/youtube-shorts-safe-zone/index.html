Article 3 — YouTube Shorts Safe Zone: Keep Your Subtitles and Hooks Visible

YouTube Shorts look simple — a vertical video, a few buttons — but Shorts hide your content in two places that catch almost every new creator. The first is the right-hand Like and Subscribe column, which covers the entire right edge. The second is the title and channel overlay at the bottom, which sits exactly where most people put their subtitles.

If your Shorts subtitles are cut off, your hook is half-hidden, or your logo disappears behind the Subscribe button, this guide will show you the exact safe zone and how to check a Short before you upload it.

The YouTube Shorts safe zone in pixels (1080 × 1920)

Shorts are 1080 × 1920, 9:16 vertical video. YouTube draws its interface over the top, and the covered areas in 2026 are:

EdgeKeep clear
Top230 px (12%) — search bar, camera icon and menu
Bottom420–470 px — video title, channel name, description and audio line
Left60–80 px — safe margin
Right180–200 px — Like, Dislike, comments, Share, Subscribe and sound column

That leaves a central safe rectangle of roughly 820 × 1,220 pixels. Your hook, your subtitles, your face and your logo belong inside it. Anything outside it is scenery.

One thing that surprises people: the right-hand column on Shorts is more aggressive than on TikTok or Reels, because it carries the Subscribe button and the channel avatar as well as the usual engagement buttons. Text in the right 200 pixels is not just at risk — it is almost certainly covered.

What covers your Shorts?

  • The right engagement column. Like, Dislike, comments, Share, Subscribe, the sound toggle and the channel avatar stack down the right edge for the full height of the video. It never goes away.
  • The bottom title overlay. Your video title, channel name and the audio track line sit at the bottom left, over a dark gradient. A long title covers more of the frame — keep titles short, and never put subtitles in the bottom 470 pixels.
  • The top bar. Search, the camera/shorts button and the three-dot menu cover the top 230 pixels.
  • The thumbnail crop. A Short's thumbnail is shown in search, on your channel page and in the Shorts shelf, often cropped to a different shape. If the thumbnail is a still from your video, make sure the subject and any title text are central — the edges get cut.
  • Related-video end screens. On the web player, Shorts can show related videos at the end. Do not put your call to action in the last second at the bottom of the frame.

Why your Shorts subtitles get cut off

Subtitles are the number-one casualty, because creators put them where subtitles have always gone: at the bottom. On Shorts, the bottom belongs to YouTube. The fixes, in order of impact:

1. Move subtitles up. Put burned-in subtitles in the middle of the frame, or in the upper half of the safe rectangle. It looks unusual at first, and it is the only place they are guaranteed visible. YouTube's auto-captions are positioned by the app, but burned-in captions from CapCut, Premiere or Descript are your responsibility.

2. Shorten the title. Your title is drawn over the bottom left of your video. A 100-character title covers far more than a 40-character one. Write the title for the overlay, not just for search: short, front-loaded, and free of words you also need on screen.

3. Keep the hook central, not top or bottom. The first three seconds decide whether someone swipes away. If the hook text is at the top (under the search bar) or at the bottom (under the title), viewers never read it. Centre it.

4. Do not put logos on the right. Channel logos, watermarks and "subscribe" prompts placed on the right edge sit directly under YouTube's own Subscribe button. Put them on the left, inside the safe area, or leave them out — the channel avatar is already on screen.

5. Check the thumbnail separately. The Shorts thumbnail is your click-through in search and on channel pages. Use the YouTube Shorts cropper to check the 16:9 thumbnail crop as well as the 9:16 video — a thumbnail that is safe in one shape is often cropped in the other.

YouTube picks a frame from your Short automatically unless you upload one, and that frame is very often the worst frame — a blink, a transition, or a shot with text half-covered. Shorts checklist

  • Hook, subtitles and logo are inside the central 820 × 1,220 safe area
  • Nothing important is in the top 230 px or the bottom 470 px
  • Nothing important is in the right 200 px
  • Subtitles sit in the middle of the frame, not at the bottom
  • Title is short and front-loaded
  • Thumbnail text is central and survives the 16:9 crop

Shorts reward the creators who respect the frame. The algorithm shows your video to people who have never heard of you; the safe zone decides whether those people can actually read what you made. Get the frame right and the content gets its chance.
