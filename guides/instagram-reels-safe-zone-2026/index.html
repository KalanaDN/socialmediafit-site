Instagram Reels has two different safe zones, and that is why so many creators get caught out. A Reel that looks perfect in the full-screen viewer can have its text chopped in half in the feed — because Instagram shows the same video in two different shapes, with two different sets of overlays, and it never warns you about either one.

This guide gives you the exact numbers for 2026, explains what covers each edge, and shows you how to check a Reel in under two minutes before you post it.

The two shapes: 9:16 viewer and 4:5 feed

Every Reel is shot at 1080 × 1920, in a 9:16 vertical frame. But most people do not first see your Reel full-screen. They see it in their feed, where Instagram crops it to 4:5 — a shorter, squarer preview, about 1080 × 1350 pixels. The top and bottom of your video are cut off in that preview, and a "Watch Reels" tap is the only way to see the rest.

So you are designing for two frames at once:

  • The Reels viewer (9:16, 1080 × 1920). Full screen, with the right-hand reaction rail, the caption at the bottom, and the top navigation.
  • The in-feed preview (4:5, 1080 × 1350). The middle slice of your video. Anything near the top or bottom of the 9:16 frame simply does not exist here.

If your hook is at the very top of the frame, feed viewers will never see it. If your call to action is at the very bottom, neither will they. The safe place for the words that matter is the middle of the frame — the part that survives both crops.

The Instagram Reels safe zone in pixels (1080 × 1920)

These are the clearances that hold up across the current Instagram app in 2026. Instagram, like TikTok, does not publish a single fixed table — the covered area shifts with caption length, audio, and whether the Reel is an ad — so these are conservative numbers. Design inside them and you clear every case:

EdgeKeep clear (Reels viewer)
Top220 px (11%) — Reels header, audio and navigation
Bottom420–460 px — caption, username, audio line and action bar
Left60–80 px — safe margin
Right185–195 px — like, comment, share and save rail

That leaves a central safe area of roughly 820 × 1,210 pixels. Keep faces, subtitles, logos, prices and calls to action inside it.

For the in-feed 4:5 preview, the rule is stricter: keep the important content inside the middle 1,350 pixels of the 1,920-pixel height — roughly from 285 pixels down from the top to 285 pixels up from the bottom. Anything outside that band is invisible until someone taps through.

What covers your text on Reels?

  • The right reaction rail. Like, comment, share, save and the audio disc run down the right edge, just like TikTok. Text that runs to the right edge goes underneath them.
  • The bottom caption gradient. Instagram fades a dark gradient up from the bottom so the caption is readable — and that gradient sits on top of your video. Your username, caption and audio title live here, and a long caption pushes the covered area higher.
  • The top bar. The Reels header and navigation cover the top 220 pixels. Hooks placed at the very top are the first thing to disappear.
  • The profile grid. Your cover is cropped again on your profile grid, to roughly 9:16 with the bottom cut, and the view count is stamped on it. Keep cover text central.
  • Ads. If you boost a Reel, Instagram adds a call-to-action button and a headline at the bottom, which tightens the safe zone further — treat a boosted Reel like a TikTok ad and keep another 100 pixels clear at the bottom.

Why your Reels text gets cut off

The most common causes, in the order we see them:

1. You designed for the viewer, not the feed. The hook is at the top, the punchline is at the bottom, and both are invisible in the 4:5 preview that most people actually see first. Put the hook in the centre.

2. Subtitles sit at the bottom. Burned-in subtitles default to the lower third, which is exactly where Instagram puts the caption. Move subtitles up into the safe rectangle, or use Instagram's own caption sticker, which the app positions for you.

3. The caption grew after you designed the frame. You placed text just above the caption, then wrote a three-line caption with hashtags. The covered area grew and swallowed it. Draft the caption before you place the text.

4. You reused a TikTok export. TikTok and Reels safe zones are close but not identical — Reels needs more clearance at the top (220 px vs TikTok's 170 px) and the in-feed 4:5 crop is unique to Instagram. A video that is safe on TikTok is not automatically safe on Reels. Check it again for Instagram.

5. Text is too close to the right rail. Anything in the right 185 pixels is under the like and share buttons. This is where prices, handles and "link in bio" prompts usually die.

A quick checklist for every Reel

  • Hook, subtitles and call to action are inside the central 820 × 1,210 safe area
  • Nothing important is in the top 220 px or the bottom 460 px
  • Nothing important is in the right 195 px
  • The hook is visible in the 4:5 in-feed preview, not just full-screen
  • The cover title survives the profile-grid crop
  • The caption was written before the text was placed, not after

Follow that list and your Reels will look the same to the person scrolling as they did to you in the editor — which is the whole point. The words that make someone stop, watch and follow should never be the words Instagram hides.
