Article 1 — Why Is My Text Cut Off on TikTok? (And How to Fix It)

Written by the SocialMediaFit team — safe-zone figures checked against the live apps, 26 November 2026.

You spent an hour on a TikTok. The hook is perfect, the captions are timed, the call to action is right there at the bottom — and then you post it, and half of it is hidden behind a username, a caption and a row of buttons. The text is not gone. TikTok is just drawing its own interface on top of your video.

This is the single most common formatting mistake on TikTok, and it is entirely preventable. The fix takes two minutes once you know where the app covers your frame.

What is actually covering your text?

TikTok does not shrink your video. It plays your full 1080 × 1920 frame and then layers its interface over the top. On a normal organic post, four things cover your content:

  • The top bar. The Following / For You tabs, the search icon and the live indicator sit across the top of the screen — roughly the top 250 pixels.
  • The right-hand rail. The profile picture, like, comment, bookmark, share and the spinning sound disc run down the right edge. Anything in the right 195 pixels is at risk, and in the lower half of the screen TikTok's own template keeps up to 300 pixels clear on the right.
  • The bottom caption block. Your username, the caption (up to four lines), the sound line and, on ads, a call-to-action button stack up from the bottom. That block can cover 460 pixels — more if your caption is long.
  • The profile grid. If someone finds your video from your profile, the grid crops your 9:16 cover to about 3:4, cutting roughly 240 pixels from the top and bottom, and stamps the view count on the bottom left.

None of this is a bug, and TikTok will not warn you about it. The app assumes you designed around it.

The TikTok safe zone in pixels (1080 × 1920)

There is no single official number, because the covered area grows as your caption gets longer. TikTok publishes downloadable safe-zone templates rather than a table, and the templates change with caption length and format. The numbers below are the conservative reading that most creator tools use in 2026 — design inside them and you will clear every caption length:

That leaves a central safe rectangle of roughly 820 × 1,210 pixels in the middle of the frame. Put your hook, your subtitles, your logo and your call to action inside that rectangle, and nothing TikTok draws will touch them. Everything outside it is decoration only — background, b-roll, scenery.

A useful rule of thumb: if your text is in the middle 50–60% of the frame, it is safe. If it touches any edge, it is not.

Why your text gets cut off even when you followed the rules

A few traps catch people who already know about the safe zone:

1. You checked it in the editor, not in the feed. CapCut, Canva and TikTok's own editor show your video without the full feed interface. The right rail and the caption block only appear when the video plays in the For You feed. Always preview in the actual feed, or in a checker that draws the real overlays.

2. Your caption grew. A one-line caption covers about 460 pixels at the bottom. Add a second line and it covers more; with a long caption and a call-to-action button, TikTok's own In-Feed ad template reserves up to 660 pixels. If your on-screen text sits just above the caption block, a longer caption will swallow it. Write the caption first, then place the text.

3. You are looking at an ad, not an organic post. In-Feed ads and Spark Ads add a headline and a call-to-action button on top of the caption. Their safe zone is tighter — TikTok's own In-Feed ad template reserves 660 pixels at the bottom. If you boost a video, check it against the ad zone, not the organic one.

4. Subtitles drift. Auto-captions sit near the bottom by default, exactly where the caption block lives. If you burn subtitles into the video, put them in the upper half of the safe rectangle, not at the very bottom of the frame.

5. The cover is a different crop. Your thumbnail is not your video. The profile grid crops it to 3:4 and covers the bottom left with the view count. Keep the cover's title in the centre, and never put the only copy at the bottom of the cover image.

How to fix it before you post (2 minutes)

  1. Upload your frame to a safe-zone checker. Open the TikTok Safe Zone Overlay tool, drop in your image or video still, and it draws the real TikTok interface — top bar, right rail, caption block — over your frame exactly where the app puts it.
  2. Move anything that is covered. Drag the text, the logo and the face into the central safe rectangle. If a line will not fit, shorten the line; do not shrink the font until it is unreadable.
  3. Shorten the caption if the bottom is crowded. Every line you cut buys back about 46 pixels at the bottom.
  4. Check the cover separately. Switch to the profile-grid view and make sure the cover title survives the 3:4 crop.
  5. Export and post. Export at 1080 × 1920, 9:16, and post. If you change the caption after posting, remember the covered area changes with it.

The mistakes that cost views

The creators who lose views to this are not careless — they are usually making one of three mistakes. First, they put the hook at the very top, where the Following / For You tabs sit. Second, they put the call to action at the very bottom, where the caption and the sound line sit. Third, they design on a desktop, where none of these overlays exist, and never look at the video on a phone before posting. Any one of those will hide the exact words that were supposed to make someone stop scrolling.

There is a simpler way to think about it. TikTok gives you the middle of the screen. The edges belong to TikTok. Design for the middle, check the edges, and your text will be visible to everyone who watches.
